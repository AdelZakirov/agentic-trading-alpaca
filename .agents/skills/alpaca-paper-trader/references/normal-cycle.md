# Normal candidate cycle

Read this reference only for a normal Stage 2 cycle that may find new trades.

1. Check the shortlist date and evidence. Do not open a new position from a missing or clearly stale shortlist.
2. Scan the whole shortlist before narrowing it. Do not stop at the first few rows, obvious overlaps, or first plausible trade. Keep a compact coverage ledger in the daily log showing which candidates advanced and grouping the rest by factual rejection reason.
3. Review every existing position and open order separately from the new-candidate funnel, even when its ticker is no longer on the shortlist. Review every row in the ghost index, loading only the individual files triggered by the memory contract.
4. Build an adaptive research funnel with deliberate exploration across signal sources, directions, sectors, catalysts, and instruments. Advance a candidate when current evidence suggests research could materially change opportunity ranking, portfolio construction, or the stock-versus-options choice.
5. Continue while expected information value remains meaningful. Stop widening only when the remaining candidates are duplicative, weak, non-actionable, insufficiently liquid, incompatible with the risk posture, unlikely to change the decision, or when the market, shortlist, or research service is unusable. Record why exploration stopped.
6. Use moneyheap for every advanced candidate. Before the first request, read [moneyheap-api.md](moneyheap-api.md) completely. Choose fundamental or technical analysis for the decision question and use both when they can resolve material ambiguity or improve a leading hypothesis. A failed request does not remove the candidate or end research on the rest of the funnel.
7. A bearish direction may reject a new long-stock order, but it must not reject the ticker by itself. If the bearish option path remains plausible, read [options-api.md](options-api.md) completely and assess it before deciding no trade.
8. After current evidence arrives, rank the researched funnel. Make separate stock and option decisions for every advanced candidate and every existing position or order. Triage-only names need a factual rejection reason, not fabricated full research.
