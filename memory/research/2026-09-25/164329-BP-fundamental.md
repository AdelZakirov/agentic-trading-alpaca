* **Macro Assumptions:** Baseline cash flows assume Brent crude holding in the $80–$95/bbl range. At current levels (~$95/bbl), BP generates surplus free cash flow well above its capital expenditure and base dividend requirements.

#### C. Edge vs. Chasing Crude Oil Directly
1. **Shareholder Yield Buffer:** Buying physical crude futures/ETFs carries roll costs (contango/backwardation risks) and zero yield. BP offers a 4.54% dividend plus substantial share repurchases.
2. **Integrated Downstream & Trading Cushion:** BP’s global trading arm and refining division provide margin protection during sudden crude pullbacks, muting pure commodity downside.
3. **Valuation Re-rating Potential:** Trading at under 9.0x forward earnings, multiple expansion offers upside independent of further commodity price spikes.

#### D. Key Risks Over a 2–6 Week Window
* **Commodity Price Correction:** A rapid diplomatic or supply-driven drop in crude below $75/bbl would compress upstream margins.
* **Financial Leverage:** With $72.69B in gross debt (95.1% debt-to-equity), BP has higher balance sheet leverage than Shell or ExxonMobil, making it more sensitive to cost of capital fluctuations.
* **Refining Margin Softening:** Any unexpected contraction in European/US crack spreads could weigh on near-term earnings delivery.
